Privacy Policy

Your questions stay yours.

Effective July 3, 2026  ·  Last updated July 24, 2026

How the GeneFox app handles information. GeneFox is developed and published by Vindhya Data Science (“we,” “us,” or “our”), and this policy applies to the GeneFox apps for iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Android.

The short version

GeneFox is a tool for exploring publicly available cancer-genomics and gene-expression data. It is built to be privacy-respecting by design:

  • We do not require an account. There is no sign-up, login, or user profile.
  • We do not require account or profile information, and we do not operate servers that receive or store your questions, searches, datasets, or results. The one thing we store is an email address you choose to submit to the optional “GeneFox updates” signup (Section 3e) — voluntary, and it unlocks nothing. The third-party services the app contacts process the limited request data described below.
  • No advertising, third-party tracking, crash reporting, Firebase Analytics product, or behavioral-usage analytics are included in the app. Firebase Remote Config does process the limited installation/configuration metadata described in Section 3d.
  • The app connects to public scientific databases and, only if you opt in, to a Google AI service, to answer your genomics questions. What is sent to each is described below.
  • GeneFox is built for researchers and other professional users. It is nonclinical and must not be used for medical advice, as a medical device, or in clinical practice. The optional Cloud Assist feature is restricted to adults 18 or older by its AI provider’s terms (Section 8).
  • Your bookmarks and saved cohorts stay on your device and, on Apple devices, sync through your own private iCloud account — we cannot see them.

1What GeneFox does

GeneFox lets you browse and visualize gene-expression and related genomic data from public research repositories — for example TCGA cohorts through the NIH Genomic Data Commons, and public datasets from NCBI’s Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). To do this, the app sends scientific identifiers and search terms (such as gene symbols and dataset accession numbers) to those public services and displays the results. These requests are made directly from your device to the data providers.

2Information we do not collect

GeneFox does not collect, request, or transmit:

  • Your name, phone number, or mailing address — and your email address, except the address you voluntarily provide to the optional “GeneFox updates” email signup (Section 3e);
  • Account credentials (there are no accounts);
  • Precise location or GPS data: GeneFox does not request location permission or add coordinates to scientific-data or AI requests. Firebase Remote Config automatically receives a country code as configuration metadata (Section 3d), which store taxonomies may treat as coarse or approximate location;
  • Contacts, photos, calendar, or files outside the app;
  • Advertising identifiers (e.g., IDFA/AAID) — the app contains no advertising or tracking technology;
  • Screen/action usage analytics or crash telemetry.

The app does not use the Firebase Analytics product, Crashlytics, Firebase Cloud Messaging, or any comparable behavioral analytics/tracking service. It does use Firebase Remote Config and Firebase Installations for the operational model setting described in Section 3d; their SDK privacy manifests classify limited, unlinked diagnostic metadata under Apple’s “Analytics” purpose. GeneFox does not display ads.

GeneFox is not intended for personal, patient, or confidential information. If you enter such information into a GEO search or an explicitly authorized Cloud Assist question, that text is transmitted to the applicable third-party service as described below.

3Information sent to third parties when you use the app

To function, GeneFox sends a limited amount of information from your device to the services below. Public biomedical requests are limited to scientific identifiers (gene symbols, dataset accession numbers, and public sample identifiers) except where you type free text. Firebase separately receives the integrity and installation/configuration metadata described in Sections 3c and 3d. All connections use encrypted HTTPS.

3a. Public biomedical data providers

When you search, browse, or open a dataset, the app queries public research resources operated by third parties, including:

  • NIH Genomic Data Commons (GDC) / TCGA — api.gdc.cancer.gov
  • NCBI Entrez / Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) — eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov (U.S. National Institutes of Health)
  • UCSC Xena — *.xenahubs.net, xena.treehouse.gi.ucsc.edu
  • EMBL-EBI Expression Atlas — www.ebi.ac.uk, ftp.ebi.ac.uk
  • ARCHS4 (Ma’ayan Lab, Mount Sinai) — maayanlab.cloud
  • Amazon Web Services (S3) — static reference files hosted for the GDC Xena hub

What is sent to these services is limited to the scientific query itself — for example a gene symbol, a dataset accession, a sample barcode, or, in the GEO search box, the text you type as a search term. GeneFox does not add an account identifier, advertising identifier, or app-specific device identifier to these public-portal queries. Like any Internet service, the provider receives standard network request metadata and handles it under its own privacy policy.

For conservative store disclosure, GeneFox treats free-text GEO search history as linked to the device because NCBI receives ordinary network metadata with the request, even though GeneFox does not add an account, advertising, or app-specific device identifier.

Because a free-text search term is transmitted to NCBI, please do not enter personal or confidential information into search fields.

3b. “Ask GeneFox” AI assistant (optional, opt-in)

GeneFox includes an optional natural-language assistant, “Ask GeneFox.” It first tries to interpret your question on your device. For harder questions it can, only with your explicit consent, send the question to Google’s Gemini model through Firebase AI Logic (a Google service) to interpret it.

  • This cloud step is opt-in. Before the first automatic cloud escalation, GeneFox asks for your consent. Choosing Try harder (interpret with cloud) after its inline disclosure authorizes that one request. If you decline or dismiss the automatic prompt, that question stays on-device.
  • What is sent when you consent: your typed question plus app-defined capability flags/tokens, the interpretation instruction, and response-schema metadata.
  • What is not automatically attached: data files, dataset titles or IDs, raw GEO keys, expression or sample values, bookmarks, saved cohorts, and prior chat history. Anything you type in the question is transmitted, so do not include personal, patient, confidential, or otherwise sensitive information.
  • Who may use it: Cloud Assist is only for adults 18 or older using it for professional or business scientific research. It is nonclinical and must not be used for medical advice, as a medical device, or in clinical practice.
  • Google handles the request under Google’s terms and privacy policy (see Section 6). Retention and possible provider product-improvement use vary with the deployed Gemini service. Under Google’s current terms, unpaid service content may be used for product improvement and may be reviewed by humans; Paid Service prompts and responses are not used to improve Google’s products, but may be logged temporarily. GeneFox does not claim a particular tier or zero retention unless that deployment is verified as release evidence. Store disclosures therefore conservatively treat Cloud Assist content as device-linked and eligible for other purposes.
  • You can withdraw standing consent at any time by turning off Cloud Assist in About/Settings. This prevents future automatic cloud interpretation requests; a later Try harder tap can still authorize its one disclosed request. Withdrawal cannot change a provider’s retention or handling of requests already sent.

3c. App integrity verification

To help ensure that Cloud Assist requests come from a genuine GeneFox app, production iPhone and iPad Release builds use Firebase App Check with Apple’s App Attest and production Android Release builds use Google Play Integrity. Developer and test builds use Firebase’s registered debug providers. Production Cloud Assist is unavailable in the native macOS app because that target has no configured production attestation provider.

App Check sends cryptographic app/device attestation and an integrity token to Google. This processing is for security and fraud prevention, not advertising or tracking. Because an integrity token accompanies a Cloud Assist request, Google may be able to associate that request with app/device integrity state. Provider registration and Firebase AI Logic enforcement are console-side controls that the installed client cannot verify; they must be confirmed independently as release evidence.

3d. Cloud configuration metadata (automatic)

Firebase-enabled iPhone/iPad and Android builds use Firebase Remote Config at startup to obtain the currently deployed Cloud Assist model name and retain a real-time listener for published configuration changes while the app runs. These configuration exchanges occur even if you never consent to send a Cloud Assist question. They do not include a question, search term, dataset, result, bookmark, or saved cohort.

For these exchanges, Firebase uses a pseudonymous Firebase Installation ID to select and deliver configuration values and receives limited app/device configuration metadata such as country and language codes, time zone, operating-system version, Firebase app ID, and bundle/package identifier. On Android, Firebase Installations also collects a Firebase user agent containing the device OS, name, model, brand and form factor, the app installer, and the names and versions of included Firebase SDKs; Google says this user agent is not linked to a user/device identifier and is used to understand platform/version adoption and maintain and improve Firebase services. Firebase’s Apple SDK privacy manifests classify limited aggregate SDK/OS metadata as Other Diagnostic Data, used for the store’s Analytics purpose, not linked to you, and not used for tracking. This store-purpose label does not mean that GeneFox includes the Firebase Analytics product or records your in-app screens, actions, searches, or analyses. Google handles this metadata and the installation identifier under Firebase’s privacy and retention controls linked in Section 6. Firebase documents that it retains a Firebase Installation ID until the Firebase customer calls the deletion API; after that call, the data is removed from live and backup systems within 180 days. The identifier is unique to an app installation and does not uniquely identify a person or physical device.

3e. Optional email signup (voluntary)

GeneFox offers an entirely optional way to receive occasional product updates. If you choose to enter your email address in the signup banner on our website or in the app’s About/Settings screen, that address is sent to and stored by Vindhya Data Science, using Google Cloud Firestore as our data processor, solely to email you GeneFox news (new features and releases). This is voluntary and gates nothing — every feature, including Cloud Assist, works whether or not you sign up.

  • What is stored: your email address, a source tag (“web” or “app”), timestamps, and a flag recording whether the App Check integrity check passed — no message content, search terms, datasets, results, or tracking identifiers. An app submission also carries a Firebase App Check integrity token (Section 3c) for abuse prevention; the token itself is verified and discarded, and only the pass/fail flag is kept with your address.
  • Abuse prevention: to rate-limit submissions we keep a short-lived counter keyed by the IP address the request arrives from, for up to one hour. It is stored separately and is never attached to your address record or used to identify you.
  • Use: to send you occasional GeneFox updates. We do not sell or share the list, and we do not use it for advertising or behavioral tracking.
  • Unsubscribe: use the Unsubscribe button on the app’s About/Settings screen at any time. Unsubscribing adds your address to a separate suppression list rather than deleting your original signup record; we only ever send to the difference between the two, so the suppression cannot be undone by a later import. Signing up again is an explicit opt-in and lifts the suppression (the record of it is kept). The app forgets your address when you unsubscribe.
  • Delete: email info@vindhyadatascience.com to have your address removed from both the signup and suppression records entirely, at any time.

4Information stored on your device and in your own cloud

  • On your device: GeneFox stores your bookmarks and pinned cohorts, a short list of recently viewed items, your AI-assistant consent choice, and cached data files that make the app faster. This information stays on your device. You can clear cached data from within the app, and uninstalling the app removes it.
  • In your own iCloud (Apple devices only): On iPhone, iPad, and Mac, your bookmarks and pinned cohorts sync through your private iCloud (CloudKit) database so they are available across your Apple devices. This data lives in your iCloud account under Apple’s control; we cannot access it. Its handling is governed by Apple’s iCloud terms and privacy policy. On Android, this data is stored locally only; you can move it between devices by exporting and importing a file yourself.

5Notifications

GeneFox does not send marketing notifications or user-visible alert notifications. On Apple devices, the private CloudKit/SwiftData bookmark sync may register with Apple’s Push Notification service and use silent remote notifications in the background to learn that data in your private iCloud database changed. These Apple-managed sync signals are used only to keep your private iCloud data current; GeneFox does not use Firebase Cloud Messaging or a marketing-notification service.

7Data security

All network connections made by GeneFox use encrypted HTTPS/TLS. Apart from the voluntary email signup described in Section 3e — email addresses you choose to submit, held in Google Cloud Firestore — GeneFox does not operate a backend that stores your data, and there is no other central store of personal information operated by Vindhya Data Science. Third-party providers may retain request data under their terms as described above.

8Age and permitted audience

GeneFox is built for researchers and other professional users. Its own content — public gene-expression and survival data from research repositories — carries no age restriction, and the app does not impose one.

Cloud Assist is the exception, and the restriction comes from the AI provider’s terms rather than from GeneFox: it is for adults 18 or older using it for professional or business scientific research. The feature is optional and opt-in, and its consent step requires you to confirm you meet that requirement. Cloud Assist is not a consumer service and must not be used for medical advice, as a medical device, in clinical practice, or for any other regulated medical use. Free-text requests sent to third-party providers remain subject to their terms and privacy policies.

9International users

The public data providers and Google services described above are operated primarily in the United States, so information sent to them (scientific identifiers, Firebase configuration metadata and, if applicable, your typed query) is processed in the United States and other countries where those providers operate.

Cloud Assist may be used only in locations where Google makes the deployed Gemini service available and where its billing requirements are satisfied. The allowed-region list, deployed Paid or unpaid service status, and Firebase App Check enforcement are external release controls that the installed app cannot prove; the publisher must verify and record them before each release. If the service is unavailable, GeneFox’s local interpretation path remains available.

10Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights under laws such as the EU/UK GDPR or the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA). Except for the voluntary email signup (Section 3e) — for which you can request access or deletion at any time by emailing us — Vindhya operates no receiving backend and generally cannot access provider-held request data. Data stored on your device is under your control (clear it in-app or uninstall the app); iCloud-synced bookmarks are managed through your Apple account. Requests sent to third parties are governed by their policies and rights processes. If you have questions about your rights, contact us using the details below.
