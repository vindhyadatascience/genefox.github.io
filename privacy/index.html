Privacy Policy

Privacy Policy for GeneFox

Effective date: July 3, 2026 · Last updated: August 21, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how the GeneFox app (“GeneFox,” the “app”) handles information. GeneFox is developed and published by Vindhya Data Science (“we,” “us,” or “our”). It applies to the GeneFox apps for iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Android.

The short version. GeneFox is a privacy-respecting tool for exploring publicly available cancer-genomics and gene-expression data:
  • A GeneFox account is optional, and the app works fully without one. Browsing, analysis, imports, exports, on-device AI interpretation, and every statistic run entirely without signing in. An account exists to sync your work between your devices and to enable the optional Cloud Assist AI rung (Section 3f). You may also optionally save an API key from your separate NCBI account as described in Section 3a.
  • We do not receive or store your questions, searches, datasets, or results. If you create an account, the contents of your projects, saved views and bookmarks are encrypted on your device before they are sent, under a key our servers never hold in usable form, and stored under blinded names (Section 3f). We can see that records exist, when they changed, and how many there are; we cannot read what is in them or what they are about. Imported data files never leave your device at all, and search history is a separate opt-in that is off unless you turn it on.
  • No advertising, third-party tracking, crash reporting, Firebase Analytics product, or behavioral-usage analytics are included in the app. Supported production Apple destinations initialize Firebase Remote Config at launch; Android leaves Firebase uninitialized until a consent-authorized Cloud Assist request. Section 3d describes the resulting configuration and installation metadata.
  • The app connects to public scientific databases and, on supported production destinations only when you opt in, to a Google AI service to interpret harder questions. What is sent to each is described below.
  • GeneFox is built for researchers and other professional users. GeneFox is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional for medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. The optional Cloud Assist feature is restricted to adults 18 or older by its AI provider’s terms (Section 8).
  • Your saved work remains in app-private storage and is not uploaded by GeneFox or automatically attached to Cloud Assist. On Apple devices, bookmarks and saved cohorts sync through your own private iCloud account, and Apple may include other app-private content in user-controlled backups or device transfers; Vindhya Data Science cannot access either. Public Android v1 disables operating-system backup and device transfer for app-owned data.

This version applies from the release that introduces GeneFox accounts. Sections 3b, 3e and 3f describe optional accounts, cloud sync and account-linked email.

This policy sits alongside the Terms of Service, which govern your use of the app and of the optional account.

1. What GeneFox does

GeneFox lets you browse and visualize gene-expression and related genomic data from public research repositories — for example TCGA cohorts through the NIH Genomic Data Commons, and public datasets from NCBI’s Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). To do this, the app sends scientific identifiers and search terms (such as gene symbols and dataset accession numbers) to those public services and displays the results. These requests are made directly from your device to the data providers.

2. Information we do not collect

GeneFox does not collect, request, or transmit:

  • Your name, phone number, or mailing address — and your email address, except an address supplied by your identity provider when you choose to create an account (Section 3e), or one you give to the optional product-update mailing (Section 3e);
  • A GeneFox password. Accounts use Sign in with Apple, Google or Microsoft, so authentication happens at your identity provider and we never see, receive or store a password. If you choose to save an external NCBI API key, it is handled as described in Sections 3a and 4;
  • Precise location or GPS data: GeneFox does not request location permission or add coordinates to scientific-data or AI requests. On supported Firebase-enabled Apple destinations, and on Android after first authorized Cloud Assist use, Remote Config receives a country code as configuration metadata (Section 3d), which store taxonomies may treat as coarse or approximate location;
  • Contacts, photos, or calendar data. GeneFox accesses a local bookmark-transfer file only after you explicitly select it through a system file or document picker;
  • Advertising identifiers (e.g., IDFA/AAID) — the app contains no advertising or tracking technology;
  • Screen/action usage analytics or crash telemetry.

The app does not use the Firebase Analytics product, Crashlytics, Firebase Cloud Messaging, or any comparable behavioral analytics/tracking service. Supported Firebase-enabled Apple destinations use Firebase Remote Config and Firebase Installations at launch for the operational model setting described in Section 3d; their SDK privacy manifests classify limited, unlinked diagnostic metadata under Apple’s “Analytics” purpose. Android uses the same narrow Firebase product families only after an authorized Cloud Assist request reaches its consent-deferred bridge. GeneFox does not display ads.

GeneFox is not intended for personal, patient, or confidential information. If you enter such information into a GEO search or an explicitly authorized Cloud Assist question, that text is transmitted to the applicable third-party service as described below.

3. Information sent to third parties when you use the app

To function, GeneFox sends a limited amount of information from your device to the services below. Public biomedical requests are limited to scientific identifiers (gene symbols, dataset accession numbers, and public sample identifiers) except where you type free text. On supported Firebase-enabled destinations, Firebase separately receives the integrity and installation/configuration metadata described in Sections 3c and 3d, subject to the platform-specific timing described there. All connections use encrypted HTTPS.

3a. Public biomedical data providers

When you search, browse, or open a dataset, the app queries public research resources operated by third parties, including:

  • NIH Genomic Data Commons (GDC) / TCGA — api.gdc.cancer.gov
  • NCBI Entrez / Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) — eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov (U.S. National Institutes of Health)
  • ARCHS4 (Ma’ayan Lab, Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai) — maayanlab.cloud, only as a raw-count fallback for GEO Series classified as expression profiling by high-throughput sequencing and identified as human or mouse
  • UCSC Xena GDC, TOIL, and PanCanAtlas hubs — gdc.xenahubs.net, toil.xenahubs.net, pancanatlas.xenahubs.net
  • Regents of the University of California / UCSC Treehouse Xena — xena.treehouse.gi.ucsc.edu, for the Treehouse Cell Line Compendium v1 and the Treehouse Tumor Compendium 25.01 PolyA
  • EMBL-EBI Expression Atlas (Apple platforms only) — www.ebi.ac.uk, ftp.ebi.ac.uk
  • Amazon Web Services (S3) — gdc-hub.s3.amazonaws.com, solely for the fixed GDC Xena HM450 probe-map request described below

What is sent to these services is limited to the scientific query itself — for example a gene symbol, a dataset accession, a sample barcode, or, in the GEO search box, the text you type and any cancer-term expansion GeneFox generates on your device to perform that search. GeneFox does not add a GeneFox account identifier, advertising identifier, or app-specific device identifier to these public-portal queries. Like any Internet service, the recipient necessarily receives standard network request metadata, which can include the source IP address, request time, TLS/HTTP protocol information, and ordinary request headers. Each recipient handles that information under its published policies.

For an ARCHS4 fallback, GeneFox sends maayanlab.cloud the exact public GSE accession, the species value human or mouse, and the complete list of public GSM accessions for that series. ARCHS4 also receives the ordinary network metadata described above. GeneFox does not add your typed GEO search text, NCBI API key, local app content, or any GeneFox account, advertising, device, or authentication identifier to this request. Immediately before this provider admission, GeneFox refreshes the public Series accession, record type, assay, organism, declared sample count, and enumerated GSM list from NCBI GEO; bookmark-transfer or caller-supplied metadata cannot authorize ARCHS4. The declared count and GSM list must agree. GeneFox accepts the returned raw-count matrix only when it covers that complete public GSM list; it never silently truncates a cohort, and it does not represent ARCHS4 raw counts as TPM.

ARCHS4 states that its provided gene-expression files are licensed under CC BY 4.0. Separately, ARCHS4 states that its data are free for non-commercial purposes and that commercial users should contact MSIP. Please cite Lachmann A et al., “Massive mining of publicly available RNA-seq data from human and mouse,” Nature Communications 9, 1366 (2018), doi:10.1038/s41467-018-03751-6. See archs4.org/help.

For CCLE cell-line expression, the app contacts only xena.treehouse.gi.ucsc.edu. UCSC/Treehouse is both the publisher/provenance source and the one network recipient for this feature. GeneFox sends that endpoint the gene symbol you request, the two fixed public Treehouse dataset names, and public sample identifiers. It does not send a GeneFox account ID, advertising ID, app-specific device ID, authentication token, or API key. The Treehouse Cell Line Compendium v1 content is sourced from CCLE RNA-seq in the NCI GDC. GeneFox requests the six fixed fields published in the exact Treehouse index — Name of cell line, TCGA Acronym, Tissue, disease, Histology, and Subtype/subcategory — for local display and user-initiated export. TCGA Acronym, Tissue, disease, Histology, and Subtype/subcategory are also available as grouping dimensions. The Treehouse sidecar attributes some fields to Cellosaurus, TCGA Acronym to GDSC, and Histology to GDSC and COSMIC. Those are upstream content provenance statements: the GDSC-derived and GDSC/COSMIC-derived fields are retrieved only from the Treehouse index, and CCLE, NCI GDC, Cellosaurus, GDSC, and COSMIC are not separately contacted by this feature. This policy does not make a blanket CC BY claim for every upstream-derived field.

For Treehouse pediatric and rare-tumor expression, the app contacts the same xena.treehouse.gi.ucsc.edu endpoint and adds no new recipient. GeneFox sends the gene symbol you request, the two fixed public Treehouse Tumor Compendium 25.01 PolyA dataset names, and public sample identifiers. It requests five fixed clinical fields — disease, pedaya, sex, age_at_dx, and source_name — for local display, stratification, and user-initiated export. The compendium also publishes study_donor_id and study_dataset_id, which are per-donor identifiers from the contributing studies; GeneFox never requests, displays, or exports either. GeneFox admits only samples whose source study is St. Jude Cloud, the Sequence Read Archive, EGA, ICGC, or the Children's Brain Tumor Network, because the TCGA and TARGET samples in that compendium are the same specimens GeneFox already serves from the GDC hub.

On the first methylation lookup for which the validated reference is not already cached, GeneFox makes one fixed HTTPS GET to https://gdc-hub.s3.amazonaws.com/download/HM450.hg38.manifest.gencode.v36.probeMap. That request contains no gene symbol, sample identifier, dataset accession, free text, or query parameter; AWS receives only the fixed object path and standard network metadata. GeneFox accepts the response only if its exact size and SHA-256 match the audited reference, then caches the parsed map locally (in backup-excluded Library/Caches on Apple platforms, where the operating system may purge it, and in memory for the Android app session).

For the limited UCSC/Treehouse recipient flow, we reviewed UCSC’s published Privacy Principles and logging policies. They state purpose limitation, collection limitation, access limited to legitimate business purposes, safeguards against unauthorized access or disclosure, and no commercial use of personal information. Based on those published commitments, Vindhya Data Science confirms that UCSC/Treehouse provides the same or equivalent protection described in this policy for the limited request information it receives. This confirmation is limited to that direct flow and those published commitments; it is not a claim that GeneFox controls UCSC logs or has a separate data processing agreement. See the UCSC links in Section 6.

A GeneFox account, where you have one, is unrelated to this: it is never sent to a data provider. Separately, if you have an NCBI account, you may choose to paste an NCBI E-utilities API key into About/Settings to raise NCBI’s request-rate limit. GeneFox works without a key. If you save one:

  • it is stored on your device in the system Keychain on Apple devices or as AES-256-GCM-encrypted ciphertext in app-private preferences on Android, with the encryption key held by Android Keystore;
  • it is sent only to NCBI E-utilities, as the api_key request parameter, and is not sent to Vindhya Data Science, Firebase, Gemini, or any other public data provider; and
  • a successful blank save in the NCBI API Key setting deletes the stored value.

Because this optional key identifies the user’s separate NCBI account, store disclosures conservatively treat it as a User ID used for app functionality, linked to the user, and not used for tracking.

For conservative store disclosure, GeneFox treats free-text GEO search history as linked because NCBI receives ordinary network metadata with the request and, when configured, the optional NCBI API key. GeneFox does not add a GeneFox account identifier, advertising identifier, or app-specific device identifier.

Because a free-text search term is transmitted to NCBI, please do not enter personal or confidential information into search fields.

3b. “Ask GeneFox” AI assistant (optional, opt-in, account required for the cloud rung)

On-device interpretation is free and needs no account. GeneFox first tries to understand your question locally, and for most questions that is the end of it — nothing leaves the device. Only the optional cloud rung requires a signed-in account, in addition to the separate consent described below. Declining an account does not reduce the app’s analysis; it removes the cloud rung and leaves the on-device path working.

GeneFox includes an optional natural-language assistant, “Ask GeneFox.” It first tries to interpret your question on your device. For harder questions it can, only with your explicit consent, send the question to Google’s Gemini model through Firebase AI Logic (a Google service) to interpret it.

Cloud Assist is available on supported production iPhone, iPad, native macOS, iPad-on-Mac, and Android execution. Production uses App Attest on supported physical iPhone/iPad hardware, DeviceCheck on supported native macOS and iPad-on-Mac execution, and Play Integrity on Android. An unsupported device, missing or mismatched Firebase configuration, unavailable attestation provider, or other failed release gate leaves Cloud Assist inactive and the local interpretation path available.

  • This cloud step is opt-in. Before the first automatic cloud escalation, GeneFox asks for your consent. Just this once authorizes only the current disclosed request and saves no standing permission. Always allow hard questions saves disclosure-versioned standing consent; Keep on-device sends nothing. Choosing Try harder (interpret with cloud) after its inline disclosure separately authorizes only that one request.
  • What is sent when you consent: your typed question plus app-defined capability flags/tokens, the interpretation instruction, and response-schema metadata.
  • What is not automatically attached: data files, dataset titles or IDs, raw GEO keys, expression or sample values, bookmarks, saved cohorts, and prior chat history. Anything you type in the question is transmitted, so do not include personal, patient, confidential, or otherwise sensitive information.
  • Who may use it: Cloud Assist is only for adults 18 or older using it for professional or business scientific research. GeneFox is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional for medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.
  • Google handles the request under Google’s terms and privacy policy (see Section 6). Retention and possible provider product-improvement use vary with the deployed Gemini service. Under Google’s current terms, unpaid service content may be used for product improvement and may be reviewed by humans; Paid Service prompts and responses are not used to improve Google’s products, but may be logged temporarily. GeneFox does not claim a particular tier or zero retention unless that deployment is verified as release evidence. Store disclosures therefore conservatively treat Cloud Assist content as device-linked and eligible for other purposes.
  • You can withdraw standing consent at any time by turning off Cloud Assist in About/Settings. This prevents future automatic cloud interpretation requests; a later Try harder tap can still authorize its one disclosed request. Withdrawal cannot change a provider’s retention or handling of requests already sent.

3c. App integrity verification

To help ensure that Cloud Assist requests come from a genuine GeneFox app, production uses Firebase App Check with Apple’s App Attest on supported physical iPhone/iPad hardware, Apple’s DeviceCheck on supported native macOS and iPad-on-Mac execution, and Google Play Integrity on Android. Developer and test builds use Firebase’s registered debug providers rather than a production provider.

App Check sends cryptographic app/device attestation and an integrity token to Google. This processing is for security and fraud prevention, not advertising or tracking. Because an integrity token accompanies a Cloud Assist request, Google may be able to associate that request with app/device integrity state. Provider registration and Firebase AI Logic enforcement are console-side controls that the installed client cannot verify; they must be confirmed independently as release evidence.

App Check token refresh, stated exactly. GeneFox disables periodic App Check token auto-refresh on every platform, and an authorized request obtains a current token on demand. There is one consequence of adding accounts that qualifies this, and we would rather write it down than let it be discovered: once you are signed in and Cloud Assist has been used, the Firebase Authentication SDK maintains your sign-in token in the background, and those internal refreshes fetch an App Check token of their own. So on a signed-in device that has used Cloud Assist, some attestation traffic occurs outside a request you made. It does not occur before sign-in, it does not occur if you never use Cloud Assist, and it carries no question text, dataset, or analysis content — only the same app/device integrity material described above. This behaviour belongs to the SDK and is not configurable by the app.

None of this prevents the provider from processing the attestation needed for a request you did make.

3d. Cloud configuration and installation metadata

Supported Firebase-enabled production Apple destinations use Firebase Remote Config at startup to obtain the currently deployed Cloud Assist model name and retain a real-time listener for published configuration changes while the app runs. These Apple configuration exchanges occur even if you never consent to send a Cloud Assist question.

Android performs only local capability preflight at launch and does not initialize Firebase then. When a consent-authorized Cloud Assist request first reaches Android’s deferred bridge, the app initializes the exact reviewed Firebase identity, Firebase Installations, Play Integrity App Check, Remote Config, and Firebase AI. Before the first AI model is selected, Android installs the reviewed local model default and waits for one Remote Config fetch-and-activate attempt. A transient fetch failure may use the already activated value or that reviewed default. Android does not register a real-time configuration listener. Periodic App Check token refresh is disabled. These configuration and integrity exchanges do not include a question, search term, dataset, result, bookmark, or saved cohort; the separately described question is sent only as part of the authorized Firebase AI request.

For these exchanges, Firebase uses a pseudonymous Firebase Installation ID and installation authorization token to select and deliver configuration values. It also receives limited app/device configuration metadata such as country and language codes, time zone, operating-system version, app version/build, Firebase app ID, bundle identifier, and SDK version. Firebase’s Apple SDK privacy manifests classify limited aggregate SDK/OS metadata as Other Diagnostic Data, used for the store’s Analytics purpose, not linked to you, and not used for tracking. On Android, store taxonomies may classify the country code as approximate location, the installation and integrity identifiers as device or other IDs, and limited OS/app/SDK information as diagnostics. These store-purpose labels do not mean that GeneFox includes the Firebase Analytics product or records your in-app screens, actions, searches, or analyses. Google handles this metadata and the installation identifier under Firebase’s privacy and retention controls linked in Section 6. Firebase documents that it retains a Firebase Installation ID until the Firebase customer requests deletion or app-installation inactivity triggers its lifecycle controls; after deletion, associated data is removed from live and backup systems within 180 days. GeneFox does not currently offer an in-app Firebase Installation deletion control and cannot reliably match a support request or email address to this provider-held pseudonymous identifier. Clearing the app’s data or uninstalling it stops that installation from using the identifier; Firebase’s documented inactivity/deletion lifecycle then governs its provider-held copy. The identifier is unique to an app installation and does not uniquely identify a person or physical device.

3e. Accounts and product-update email

Creating an account is optional and gates none of the app’s analysis. It is required only for cloud sync and for the optional Cloud Assist AI rung described in Section 3b.

How you sign in. Accounts use Sign in with Apple, Google or Microsoft through Firebase Authentication. Your password is handled entirely by that provider; we never receive or store one. From the sign-in we receive a stable account identifier, and an email address and display name if your provider chooses to share them. Sign in with Apple’s Hide My Email gives us a relay address instead of your real one, and the app works identically either way — we do not ask you to reveal a real address to use it.

What an account record contains. The account identifier; the provider(s) you linked; the email and display name your provider shared, where it did; the wrapped recovery key, if you create one (Section 3f); and the encrypted records described in Section 3f.

Where your choices are kept. On the device where you made them, not in the account. So turning search history off on your phone does not travel to your laptop as a setting — but it does delete what is already on the server, which every device shares. The effect is account-wide even though the switch is not.

Abuse prevention that comes with sign-in, on Android. Firebase Authentication on Android links Google’s reCAPTCHA SDK, and it cannot be separated from it — the Auth library calls into reCAPTCHA as it initialises. Where it runs, it sends app and device signals to Google to distinguish a real sign-in from an automated one. It is not advertising, not analytics, and it is not used to profile you; it exists so that someone cannot script sign-in attempts against the service.

Two things we did about it, so you can judge the rest: reCAPTCHA also declared an Android permission to read Google Services Framework settings, and GeneFox removes that permission from the app — nothing here uses phone-number sign-in, which is what those signals are mainly for. And it is inert until you sign in: an account is optional, and a user who never creates one never invokes it.

Product-update email is a separate, explicit choice. At account creation you are asked — clearly, and not pre-ticked — whether you want occasional product updates. Declining changes nothing about the account, and you can change your mind at any time in Settings. This replaces the earlier standalone email signup.

  • What is stored: the address, whether you subscribed, where the agreement came from, the version of the disclosure you saw, and the relevant timestamps. We do not attach message content, scientific searches, datasets, or analysis results.
  • We confirm before we mail. An address that has not completed a confirmation step is not eligible for a mailing, and an unconfirmed signup cannot reactivate a suppression.
  • Unsubscribing outlives your account. When you unsubscribe, we keep an irreversible one-way hash of the address — with no account identifier attached — so that we can honour the request permanently. This is deliberate: if we deleted that record along with your account, re-importing any old list could start mailing you again, which is exactly what unsubscribing exists to prevent. The retained value cannot be used to contact you or to reconstruct your address; it can only answer “do not send to this.”

3f. Cloud sync (what signing in turns on)

Sync exists so your work follows you between your own devices, including between an iPhone and an Android phone. It is off until you sign in — and signing in is what turns it on. There is no second switch, and we would rather say so than imply a step that does not exist: syncing is what an account is for. If you would prefer your work never left the device, do not create one. Everything in the app still works.

Encrypted before it leaves your device. The contents of your projects — names, notes, saved views, the identifiers of the cohorts and datasets you saved, and your searches if you turn those on — are encrypted on your device with AES-256-GCM. The key is wrapped to your devices; our servers store only wrapped copies and never hold it in usable form.

The names records are stored under are blinded too, which is a separate problem from encrypting them and has to be solved separately. Projects and analysis recipes are stored under random identifiers that never meant anything. Saved datasets and pinned cohorts cannot be: two of your devices have to arrive at the same record for the newer edit to win, so the name has to be derived from what the record is. Those names are hashed under a key derived from yours before they are sent. The consequence is the point — a dataset you saved is not readable from our database, and the same dataset saved by two different people produces two unrelated names, so there is nothing there to join you on.

What we can still see, stated plainly: that an account has records, how many, when they last changed, and the ordering key that keeps your project list in the same order on every device. Removing that would require sending decoy traffic, which we do not consider proportionate. We cannot see what any record contains, or what it is about.

What does not sync. Data files you import never leave your device. These can be unpublished or patient-derived, so rather than a per-dataset opt-in that could be misread or mis-set, this version has no mechanism to upload them at all. Your searches are a separate opt-in and are off unless you turn them on; view history does not sync.

Losing access. Because we cannot read your data, we cannot recover it for you. Three things stand between you and losing it, and they cover different failures:

  • Replacing a device inside one ecosystem needs nothing from you. Your key is also wrapped to the platform’s own synced keychain — iCloud Keychain, Android Block Store — both of which are end-to-end encrypted by the platform and restore onto the new device by themselves.
  • Moving between ecosystems — an iPhone to an Android phone — uses a transfer code shown on a device you still have and typed into the new one. The keychains above do not cross that boundary, and running on both is the point of the app, so this is an ordinary path rather than a recovery one.
  • Losing every device needs the optional recovery code, which is the only case where nothing you own can help. It is 24 characters, shown to you once, and you can copy or export it at that moment. We store a copy of your key wrapped under it; because the code itself was never transmitted, that stored copy is not something we can open. There is no “show it to me again” — recovering the code from what we hold is precisely the attack the design refuses. If you lose it, create a new one from a device that still works, which replaces the old one.

If you lose your identity provider account itself, we cannot restore access: without the provider there is no way to authenticate you, and we will not weaken that.

Deleting. Deleting your account removes the account record, its keys and its encrypted records. Deletions propagate to your other devices; a device that has been offline for a long time may reintroduce a record it never learned was deleted, so deletion markers are kept for 180 days for that purpose and then removed. The unsubscribe suppression hash described in Section 3e is the one thing that intentionally remains.

4. Information stored on your device and in your own cloud

  • In private storage on your device: GeneFox stores bookmarks and pinned cohorts; recently viewed items; where supported, saved workspace definitions and Report Builder drafts, captured figures, and report settings; app settings, preferences, and consent choices; and downloaded or generated caches that make the app faster.
  • Recreatable caches: On Apple platforms, downloaded GEO data, Xena query results, and the validated methylation-reference map live in backup-excluded Library/Caches, where the operating system may purge them. Android uses cache or session storage for recreatable scientific data. After a cache is cleared or purged, GeneFox downloads or rebuilds the applicable public data when it is next needed.
  • In your GeneFox account, once you sign in (Section 3f): encrypted copies of your projects, saved views, bookmarks, pinned cohorts and analysis-recipe metadata, together with the unencrypted bookkeeping that lets your devices reconcile them — modification times, ordering keys, and deletion markers. The names the records are stored under are blinded, as Section 3f describes. Imported data files are excluded entirely.
  • Apple backups and device transfers: Apple may include app-private files, workspaces, Report Builder content, settings, preferences, and other app data in a user-controlled iCloud Backup, Finder/computer backup, or device-to-device transfer. These are operating-system-managed copies under the user’s and Apple’s controls; Vindhya Data Science cannot access them. Their existence is not collection by GeneFox or Vindhya Data Science.
  • Android backup: Public Android v1 disables operating-system cloud backup and device-to-device transfer for app-owned files and preferences. Its explicit bookmark export/import feature remains the supported way to move those records.
  • Optional NCBI API key: If you choose to save one, it is stored in the system Keychain on Apple devices or as AES-256-GCM-encrypted ciphertext in app-private preferences on Android, with the encryption key held by Android Keystore. A successful blank save in About/Settings deletes the stored key; on Android it removes both the encrypted preference and its Keystore entry. Apple Keychain items can persist after app uninstallation, so clear the key in the app before uninstalling if you want to ensure it is removed.
  • Optional updates address: after a successful in-app signup request, the app saves the submitted address in app-private preferences only so the installation can offer a one-tap unsubscribe. A successful unsubscribe clears that active local copy. Removing the app clears the active preference, but a pre-existing Apple backup may retain or later restore it under Apple’s controls as described above. Apple Keychain is not used for the updates address.
  • Files you choose: GeneFox reads a bookmark-transfer file only after you select it through a system file or document picker; it does not scan your files. Reports, workspaces, or bookmark files leave active private app storage only when you explicitly invoke an export or share action, subject to the Apple-managed backup and transfer copies described above.
  • Removal: You can delete saved report/workspace content with the applicable in-app controls and clear GEO downloads and Xena query caches in the app. Deleting content or uninstalling removes it from the active app storage and preferences. A pre-existing Apple backup may persist or later restore a copy under Apple’s backup-retention and deletion controls; manage those copies through your Apple account, device, or computer backup settings. The Apple Keychain exception is described above.
  • In your own iCloud (Apple devices only): On iPhone, iPad, and Mac, your bookmarks and pinned cohorts sync through your private iCloud (CloudKit) database so they are available across your Apple devices. This intentional sync is separate from the operating-system backups described above. The data lives in your iCloud account under Apple’s control; we cannot access it. Its handling is governed by Apple’s iCloud terms and privacy policy. On Android, this data is stored locally only; you can move it between devices by exporting and importing a file yourself.

5. Notifications

GeneFox does not send marketing notifications or user-visible alert notifications. On Apple devices, the private CloudKit/SwiftData bookmark sync may register with Apple’s Push Notification service and use silent remote notifications in the background to learn that data in your private iCloud database changed. These Apple-managed sync signals are used only to keep your private iCloud data current; GeneFox does not use Firebase Cloud Messaging or a marketing-notification service.

10. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights under laws such as the EU/UK GDPR or the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA). If you have an account, you can delete it from within the app or by emailing us, which removes the account, its keys and its encrypted records; you can turn search history off, which also deletes the searches already stored; and you can export your projects at any time as a .genefox file, which is a plain readable archive rather than a request you have to make of us. Because your records are encrypted with a key we do not hold, we cannot produce their contents for you in a support request — the export is how you get them, and it works whether or not we are involved. Two things this version does not offer, said plainly rather than left to be discovered: there is no way to sign a single device out remotely — signing out happens on the device itself, and deleting the account is what revokes everything at once — and apart from search history, the sync categories cannot be turned off individually. Signing out or deleting the account are the controls that exist. One retention we will not undo: the one-way unsubscribe hash in Section 3e, which carries no account identifier and exists so that a deletion cannot accidentally resubscribe you later. For the product-update mailing you can request access, suppression, correction, or verified deletion by emailing us. Beyond that, Vindhya generally cannot access provider-held request data. Active app storage is under your control through the in-app removal actions described in Section 4; Apple-managed backups and device-transfer copies are managed through your Apple account, device, or computer backup settings and are not accessible to Vindhya Data Science. Save a blank NCBI API key before uninstalling if you want to ensure the Apple Keychain copy is removed. Vindhya cannot reliably match a support request to a provider-held Firebase Installation ID; clear the app’s data or uninstall it to stop that installation from using the identifier, after which Firebase’s documented lifecycle applies. Bookmarks previously synced through your Apple account remain managed there; the account-based sync in Section 3f replaces that path and works across platforms. Requests sent to third parties are governed by their policies and rights processes. If you have questions about your rights, contact us using the details below.
